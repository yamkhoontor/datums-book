Datums, Coordinate Systems and Map Projections

Companion apps

One app for each chapter, with a button for each worked example

Open any app below in a web browser. Each runs entirely on this computer, with no internet connection and nothing to install. Change the values in the boxes and the results and drawing update at once. Every app opens with the book's example marks S, J and B, and has a button for each worked example in its chapter.

App 1 · Chapter 1One point, many coordinates
The same physical mark described in six ways. Only the numbers change; the mark stays where it is.
App 2 · Chapter 2The ellipsoid and its curvature
How the size and flattening of the ellipsoid set the length of a second of arc, wherever the work is.
App 3 · Chapter 3From latitude and longitude to X, Y, Z and to east, north, up
The line from S to J, computed three ways: as a Cartesian vector, in local east-north-up, and on the ellipsoid.
App 4 · Chapter 4GNSS height to reduced level
H = h − N. Fit a local geoid surface to benchmarks with both a GNSS height and a levelled height, then use it at a new point.
App 5 · Chapter 5Old datum, new datum: where is the 1970s sea wall?
A position read from an old Kertau-based plan, moved onto the modern geocentric datum by two published transformations.
App 6 · Chapter 6One mark, several ITRF realisations
How far apart are ITRF2020, ITRF2014, ITRF2008, ITRF2005 and ITRF2000 at the same place and the same epoch?
App 7 · Chapter 7A moving Earth: how far has the mark travelled?
Velocity of a point on the Sunda plate from its Euler pole, and the distance travelled between two epochs.
App 8 · Chapter 8From a GNSS campaign to SVY21
Bring a mark positioned in ITRF2020 today onto SVY21, by measuring the shift at SVY21 control marks observed in the same campaign.
App 9 · Chapter 9The seven-parameter transformation, one parameter at a time
See what each translation, rotation and the scale do to points across the project area, and what a wrong sign convention costs.
App 10 · Chapter 10Fitting a site grid to SVY21 by least squares
Four parameters (scale, rotation, two shifts) from common points, with residuals, a check point, and a deliberate blunder.
App 11 · Chapter 11One mark, three countries
A mark positioned in ITRF2020 in a common campaign, expressed on SVY21 (Singapore), GDM2000 (Malaysia) and SRGI2013 (Indonesia), and on each country's grids.
App 12 · Chapter 12How much does a Transverse Mercator grid distort?
Scale factor, convergence and the arc-to-chord correction, as the central scale factor and the distance from the central meridian change.
App 13 · Chapter 13SVY21 grid converter, with a check against SiReNT
Latitude and longitude to SVY21 northing and easting and back, with scale factor and convergence, tested on published stations.
App 14 · Chapter 14One line, five grids
The line S–J on SVY21, UTM 48N, the Johor Cassini grid, the Peninsula RSO and Web Mercator, compared with the true line on the ellipsoid.
App 15 · Chapter 15Grid distance or ground distance?
Reduce a measured distance to the grid, or turn a design (grid) distance into the length to set out on the ground.
App 16 · Chapter 16Why don't my coordinates agree? A diagnostic aid
Compare the coordinates you expected for a mark with those you received. The size and direction of the difference usually tell you the cause.
